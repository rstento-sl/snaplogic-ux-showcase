Who opens API Management

Every finding below comes back to the same thing: the product opens on a page built for someone who already knows it. Two customer situations arrive at that page, and a variant inside each one splits them again.

Case 1 — A Classic API Management customer subscribes to APIM 3.0, or starts a trial

Arrives fluent in the old product. Has existing assets.

They know Proxies, API versions, the Developer Portal, and subscriptions. The new product says Service, and nothing maps the old vocabulary to the new — so they can't tell whether a page is empty or simply unfamiliar.

Their real first task is migration, but the left nav puts Migration Tool below Manage, under a section heading that sits over a single link and never says what migrates to what. They also see two entries named API Manager: the product itself, and the Classic one they recognize — so the familiar click leads out of the product they're evaluating. Nothing says whether Classic is deprecated, whether both run side by side, or whether migrating affects live traffic.

Case 2 — A new customer subscribes to APIM 3.0, or starts a trial

No history with the old product. No assets yet.

They don't see the Classic section in the nav at all, so that ambiguity never reaches them. What reaches them instead is the emptiest possible version of the product: no Services, and therefore no rows — because the catalog lists only Project Spaces and Projects that contain Services.

Their first impression is a page headed Catalog Stats, five zeroes, three filters, and nothing else. Nothing defines a Service, and nothing says what to do first.

The variant inside both cases

Somebody on the team goes first — creating a Service, or migrating a Classic Proxy or API version. Everyone else arrives afterward, into what that person built. The two roles need opposite things from the same screens.

The first person on the team

Faces a browsing interface with nothing to browse. The only useful action is Add service, parked at the top right of a card below the stats, which never says which Project the new Service lands in.

They also choose the Project Space and Project structure everyone after them inherits — decided blind, at the moment they know least about the product.

Everyone who follows

Has the opposite problem: find my Service inside a structure somebody else chose. This is where missing search, sort, and counts hurt, and where environment-wide statistics say nothing useful — “48 unpublished” is not a fact about their work.

Permissions help them more than they expect: the list shows only what they can access. But nothing says so, which leaves its own question — is my team's Service missing, or can I simply not see it?
